# lib/ntt

Polynomial multiplication modulo P = 998244353. API and usage: the headers of `ntt.hpp`
(`ntt::Convolution`, any sizes) and `product.hpp` (`ntt::Product`, factors of at most half the
transform, faster). Tests: `lib/ntt/test.cpp`. Kernels: `kernels.hpp` and `product_kernels.hpp`,
generated by `gen_kernels.py` and `gen_product_kernels.py` (do not edit).

## Design

- One cyclic transform of length n = 2^lg >= N + M - 1, 64 <= n <= 2^25. The transform splits
  x^n - 1 down to n/8 leaves x^8 - w and multiplies each pair of leaves directly (64 products,
  Montgomery reduction). Only roots of order n/8 <= 2^22 are needed; P - 1 = 119 * 2^23.
- Radix-4 groups, depth first: group k at stride h holds 4h vectors (a polynomial mod
  X^4 - r[k]^2, X = x^(8h)); its children are 4k + t. Subtrees of at most 256 vectors (8 KiB per
  array) run level by level ("tiles"). The bottom level (h = 1) is fused with the leaf products and
  the first inverse level, and batch j + 1's forward half is interleaved with batch j's products.
- Top level: n/8 = 4^j starts with one radix-4 group, n/8 = 2 * 4^j with a radix-2 level. If an
  input's upper half is zero (always true for N, M <= n/2), the radix-2 level is a copy and the
  radix-4 level reads only the lower half. The final scale (n/8)^-1 2^32 is fused into the last
  inverse level.
- Arithmetic: Shoup multiplication by twiddles: w < P with q = floor(w 2^32 / P); for any
  x < 2^32, x w - floor(x q / 2^32) P is in [0, 2P). Forward values stay < 4P, inverse < 2P;
  min(x, x - 2P) reduces lazily. The odd lanes' quotients come from a load 4 bytes further on
  (no shift), so the forward kernels read 4 bytes past their last vector.
- Twiddle tables: entry k = product of roots[j] over the set bits j of k (roots[j] of order
  2^(j + 2)), so entry 2k is a square root of entry k and entry 2k + 1 = sqrt(-1) times it.
  Blocks of 8 values then their 8 quotients; built per call, n/16 entries each way, quotient of v
  = (v 2^32 mod P) * (-1/P) mod 2^32.
- Leaf weights of group k: r[2k], -r[2k], r[2k+1], -r[2k+1]; the quotient of P - w is ~q(w).
- Memory: a, b and the tables in one mapping, 2 MiB aligned, MADV_HUGEPAGE, faulted on first touch.
  a and b are n + 16 words apart, so equal offsets fall in different cache sets.

## Kernels

`gen_kernels.py` builds each kernel as a dataflow graph of AVX2 instructions, list-schedules it on
a Zen 3 model (4 vector pipes, multiplies and shifts on 2 each, 2 loads, 1 store per cycle) under
a register budget, allocates ymm0-15 by linear scan and emits one asm statement per loop.

| Kernel | Butterflies per iteration | Schedule knobs |
|---|---|---|
| `forward` (h >= 16) | 2 | deterministic |
| `forward_pair` (h = 4, a and b) | 1 + 1 | seed 129, margin 1, 3 loads per cycle |
| `inverse` | 2 | seed 138, modeled load latency 16 |
| `forward_identity`, `inverse_identity`, `scale_radix2` | 2 | deterministic |
| `bottom_first`, `bottom_last`, `bottom_both` | one batch of 4 vectors | window 12; seeds 0, 7, 540; `both` interleaves its stages with a shift of -0.05 |

The knobs come from QPoly exploration 009, where fixed-knob families were timed on EPYC 7763 and
the fastest kept; `llvm-mca` rankings did not predict the hardware. The generator is a rewrite of
that exploration's `gen_asm.py`, reduced to the selected kernels; its output equals the selected
QPoly kernels instruction for instruction (checked 2026-10-09 against the QPoly
convolution_mod_large deliverable).

## Product

`ntt::Product` (`product.hpp`) is `ntt::Convolution` for factors of at most half the transform
length, lg >= 9, with three changes from convolution_mod's rounds 1-5
(`problems/convolution/convolution_mod/notes.md` has the measurements):
- Bottom stage (`product_kernels.hpp`, `gen_product_kernels.py`): two groups per always-inlined asm
  statement; leaf weights y, -y, z, -z are the group's own twiddles (no weight array; the windows of
  t = 1, 3 hold P - canonical(y A_t)); each coefficient of B broadcast once. On Zen 3: 145 -> 127
  cycles per group (-13%). Subtrees need at least 16 vectors, so tiles start at even groups.
- nv = 2 * 4^j (even lg): `forward_radix8` reads each factor's lower half once and writes the first
  radix-4 group of both halves; `inverse_top` fuses `inverse_identity`, the upper half's `inverse`
  and `scale_radix2`, with the scale folded into the twiddles (9 Shoup products per column, not 13).
- nv = 4^j (odd lg): `ntt::Convolution`'s sparse radix-4 top level and scaled inverse.
- Neither top level reads words length() / 2 and up of a or b: callers may keep data there until
  `multiply()` (mul_modp_convolution keeps its input pairs there).
- `Product(n, m, extra)` maps `extra` more bytes after the tables, for the caller's text buffer
  (convolution_mod: no page faults of its own).

## Measurements

AMD EPYC 7B13 (`lc-amd`), GCC 15.2 in the pinned image, judge flags, 2026-10-09. Whole programs on
the official tests (`tools/judge.py bench`, slowest 3 cases), against the QPoly programs behind our
records:

| Problem | QPoly program | ms | This | ms | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| convolution_mod (31 rounds) | exploration 011 (408716, judged 14 ms) | 14.73 | lib/ntt + lib/io | 14.44 | 0.979 |
| convolution_mod_large (11 rounds) | exploration 014 (408888, judged 452 ms) | 426.7 | lib/ntt + lib/io | 424.6 | 0.994 |

Phases on fft_killer_04 of convolution_mod_large (ms, from `main`, 3 of 4 runs): parse 64.9 → 64.4,
transform 202.6 → 201.4, output 142.7 → 144.3, exit 15 (QPoly's not measured separately). The
transform matches QPoly's; the output's formatter is GCC's code (34.7 ms in memory for 2^25
values, QPoly's hand-written asm 32.3).
